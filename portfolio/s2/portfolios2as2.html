S2AS2: Japan Travel Archive

↗ View the Live Archive

This project serves as a digital archive of physical transit and entry stubs collected during an architectural research trip to Japan in early 2026. Traveling from Philadelphia to sites across Tokyo, Kyoto, and Naoshima Island, the collection functions as a spatial and temporal map of the journey. By utilizing CSS interaction states, the site transforms flat, scanned ephemera into interactive portals that reveal the specific memories and spatial qualities associated with each location.

Technical Execution

Interaction via Selectors

The core mechanic relies heavily on CSS pseudo-classes (:hover, :active, :focus) rather than JavaScript. By instructing the user to "Hold to view," the site utilizes the :active state on the .ticket-item containers to trigger the visibility of a hidden .popout-overlay. Releasing the click naturally reverts the DOM element to its hidden state, creating a tactile, physical interaction model.

Viewport Accommodations

To serve alternate user experiences across devices, the site implements responsive design strategies. A combination of viewport ratio properties (vw/vh) ensures the ticket stack scales proportionately, while @media queries reorganize the flex/grid layouts so the overlay images remain legible and properly framed whether viewed on a desktop monitor or a mobile screen.

Optimization & Constraint

Loading 10 distinct images (5 ticket scans, 5 high-resolution photographs) required strict asset optimization to ensure the entire page weight remained under the 1MB assignment limit. Images were compressed and sized specifically for their maximum rendered dimensions within the DOM.

Semantic Metadata

Rather than treating the alt text as a mere accessibility afterthought, this project utilizes metadata as an invisible layer of narrative. The alt text for the hidden photographs reads as a poetic travel log—describing Tadao Ando's concrete, the blur of the Shinkansen, and the urban scale of Tokyo—offering a rich, alternative textual experience.

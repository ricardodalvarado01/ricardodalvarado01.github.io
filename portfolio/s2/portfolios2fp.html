S2FP: The Weight of the Present

↗ View Full Screen ↗ View Source Code

Concept

This is a constructivist-inspired clock designed to combat the anxiety of the future inherent in traditional timekeeping. While standard clock faces project the entirety of the day at once, forcing the user into a constant, capitalist calculation of remaining time, this design utilizes a heavy, subtractive black mask to collapse the viewer’s focus into the "now."

Inspired by the radical geometric language of El Lissitzky, the clock features 12-point internal tick marks that remain hidden behind a 330-degree rotating aperture. This shutter grants access only to the internal data of the current hour, creating a sense of focus that protects the user from the clutter of the full day; the user is allowed to exist only in the present.

The Red Hand

Inspired by Constructivist posters, the red minute hand acts as a "needle" or "pointer." It provides a sharp, aggressive contrast to the heavy black void of the hour mask.

The Orbit

The second hand is reduced to a single, orbiting point. Operating like a satellite, it represents the constant, relentless pulse of life moving around the rigid structure of the present.

Process & Sketches

Sketch 01 I knew I wanted to work with the overlapping and masking of shapes, but in this concept, the shaded circles didn't show time as precisely.
Sketch 02 While the first concept was about masking and privacy, this second version explores transparency and intersection. I wanted to see what was gained by allowing the three time-loops to occupy the same space simultaneously.
Sketch 03 This sketch explores hierarchy through line weight. In architecture, we use line weights to distinguish between section cuts (heavy) and detail lines (light). I've applied that same logic to time.
Sketch 04 A combination of a couple of the concepts, moving towards the main concept of isolating the present. I felt that I didn't need all the seconds, just a pulsating one to further enhance the present.
